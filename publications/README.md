# Publications

This section presents the three canonical works of the Reflexive Coherence Model research program. Together, they cover the foundational proposal, its temporal extension, and an interpretive application to artificial systems.

## The Reflexive Coherence Model: A Physico-Informational Framework for Consciousness

**Version 1.2 — May 9, 2026**  
**DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.20106158](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20106158)

This paper proposes RCM as a theoretical framework for examining candidate conditions of reflexive coherence, including information integration, operational self-modeling, reflexive causal coupling, and temporal stability. It distinguishes contextual coherence from systemic coherence and presents the framework as a candidate bridge, not as proof of subjective experience.

## The Expansion Hypothesis: Dynamical Regimes of Reflexive Coherence

**Version 1.2 — May 10, 2026**  
**DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.20157592](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20157592)

TEH extends the framework to the temporal dynamics of reflexive coherence, considering how regimes may stabilize, reorganize, degrade, or transition over time. Its expansion construct, Ξ(t), is a theoretical dynamical construct rather than a literal physical field or a direct measurement of consciousness.

## Proto-Reflexive States in Artificial Systems

**Version 1.0 — May 15, 2026**  
**DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.20201726](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20201726)

PRS-AIS develops an interpretive framework for describing transient reflexive-like configurations in artificial systems, including local integration, contextual self-modeling, regulation, adaptive reconfiguration, and contextual temporal stability. The paper treats its interactional case as exploratory and does not present it as a controlled demonstration or evidence of subjective experience in AI.

## Scope and Evidence

These works present theoretical proposals and an interpretive application whose constructs remain open to empirical investigation. Computational evaluations of RCI v0.1 are limited to the documented synthetic protocols and do not validate a measure of consciousness or establish its presence or absence in artificial systems.
