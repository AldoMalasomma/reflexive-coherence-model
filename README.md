# Reflexive Coherence Model

## A Physico-Informational Framework for Consciousness

The Reflexive Coherence Model (RCM) is a theoretical research program exploring candidate organizational conditions for reflexive coherence. Its proposed relationships remain open to empirical investigation; RCM does not demonstrate subjective experience in biological or artificial systems.

## Research Overview

The program asks how information integration, operational self-modeling, causal coupling, and temporal stability can be described and investigated together. Its guiding questions include:

- How can these properties be operationalized without treating them as evidence of experience?
- What distinguishes transient organization from a persistent regime?
- How should contextual support be distinguished from system-level persistence?
- Which observations and interventions could test these proposed relationships?

These are research questions, not established findings.

## Core Concepts

- **Information integration:** relationships among informational components of a system across multiple levels.
- **Operational self-modeling:** a system’s functional representation of aspects of its own state or causal organization.
- **Reflexive causal coupling:** proposed bidirectional causal influence between a system’s organization and its operational self-model.
- **Temporal stability:** persistence or reorganization of these relations over a finite interval, including a distinction between contextual support and system-level persistence.

These concepts are theoretical and operational candidates whose measurement and explanatory value require further investigation.

## Research Evolution

The program has developed through three complementary contributions:

- **RCM** — Initially formulated in 2025 and refined in May 2026 (v1.2), it explores candidate organizational conditions for reflexive coherence.
- **The Expansion Hypothesis (TEH)** — Introduced in December 2025 and refined in May 2026 (v1.2), it considers the temporal dynamics of reflexive coherence, including expansion, stabilization, reorganization, and contraction.
- **Proto-Reflexive States in Artificial Systems (PRS-AIS)** — Published in May 2026 (v1.0), it offers an interpretive framework for transient reflexive-like configurations in artificial systems without claiming evidence of subjective experience.

Together, these works form an evolving theoretical research program.

## From Theory to Computational Investigation

The **Reflexive Coherence Index (RCI)** is a proposed operational approach for examining selected properties of RCM. RCI v0.1 is an initial computational implementation explored by **Project Janus** using deterministic synthetic environments, controlled interventions, ablation protocols, and reproducible decision traces. It is not a validated measure of consciousness or subjective experience.

Two campaigns have been documented:

- **M1** provided limited support for operational criteria under a fixed synthetic protocol, while revealing parameter sensitivity and invalid measurement regions.
- **M2** exposed significant limitations in metric validity and cross-environment comparability. Forensic analysis identified methodological issues that prevent interpreting the outcome as a general falsification or validation of RCM.

These investigations help refine the operational methodology and identify testable limitations. They do not establish the presence or absence of consciousness in artificial systems.

## Current Research Status & Limitations

RCM and TEH remain theoretical frameworks requiring further empirical and computational development. PRS-AIS is interpretive and observational: its exploratory interactional case is not a controlled experimental demonstration.

The RCI, M1, and M2 findings are limited to the operational methods and synthetic environments described above. They do not validate RCI as a measure of consciousness or establish whether artificial systems have subjective experience.
