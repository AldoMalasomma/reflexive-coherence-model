# Project Janus Experiments

## Overview

Project Janus is the computational research component of the RCM program. Its M1 and M2 campaigns explored RCI v0.1 as a provisional operational approach in controlled synthetic systems. They examine properties of system organization; they do not test for consciousness.

## Experimental Setup

The documented campaigns use deterministic synthetic environments and frozen protocols. Their methods include control conditions, baseline comparisons, controlled interventions, ablations, matched counterfactual runs, and defined perturbations. These methods examine whether measured functional patterns change under specified conditions.

## M1 — Initial Operational Evaluation

M1 reported limited support for its operational criteria under a fixed synthetic protocol. The result is specific to that protocol and does not establish general validity for RCI or RCM. The robustness audit found parameter sensitivity, particularly in perturbational recovery, as well as regions with invalid measurements.

## M2 — Cross-Environment Evaluation

M2 tested the operational approach across deterministic synthetic environments. It exposed limitations in metric validity and cross-environment comparability. Of 600 paired comparisons, 66 were valid; the primary causal contrasts had no valid pairs and therefore could not be evaluated.

## Forensic Interpretation

Under its frozen rules, M2 retained the historical procedural label “falsified across environment families.” The subsequent forensic analysis concluded that the outcome was dominated by metric/control incompatibility and non-evaluable primary contrasts, rather than valid theory-level negative evidence. M2 therefore does not establish a general falsification or validation of RCM.

## Limitations & Next Steps

Both campaigns concern RCI v0.1 in synthetic environments. M1’s limited support and M2’s operational findings do not validate RCI as a measure of consciousness or establish the presence or absence of subjective experience in artificial systems.

The forensic review marks M3 as not ready. Further work requires revised methodology, including metric-specific validity conditions, compatible controls, explicit handling of non-evaluable results, and a preregistered approach to missing evidence and recovery specificity.
